Abstract: "[Summarized with Claude AI] This nursing simulation scenario follows the handoff and care of Yvette Ogum, a 34-year-old female admitted following a heroin overdose that was reversed with naloxone in the ER. During the shift report, the outgoing nurse labels the patient as a 'drug seeker' and 'high maintenance,' advising avoidance—an attitude the oncoming nurse, Sharon, deliberately rejects, noting that such patients are frequently under-medicated. Sharon performs a thorough, compassionate assessment of the patient, who presents with severe back pain, anxiety, tremors, repeated vomiting, diarrhea, sweating, and a high COWS (Clinical Opiate Withdrawal Scale) score. Recognizing the signs of active opioid withdrawal, Sharon advocates for the patient by contacting the physician, Dr. Lopez, to obtain anti-anxiety and additional pain medication beyond the scheduled ibuprofen, while carefully accounting for the patient's aspirin allergy. As symptoms are managed and the COWS score drops, the patient—going through a divorce and struggling with her relationship with her children—begins to feel treated as a human being. The scenario powerfully demonstrates the dangers of stigmatizing language in handoffs, the clinical importance of withdrawal assessment, patient advocacy, therapeutic communication, and connecting patients with ongoing support and rehabilitation resources."
