Abstract: >-
  Today, a hot summer day (well, actually we're in May but it looks like an
  extremely hot summer...), I came across a tagline about education. 

  It all started with Aristotle :


  > "For the things we have to learn before we can do them, we learn by doing them."


  I thought, there must have been a few slogans, taglines, citations or whatsoever connected to experiential learning, situational learning and so on.

  So did I ask various generative AI (chatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Mistral,...) to make a list of citations. 

  My original objective was to give even higher motivation to promote Experiential Learning by inspiring all of us - we, the experiential learning community.

  A simple list, nothing else.


  Immediately came the most influential experiential learning theorists: Dewey and Kolb. Their citations laid out the foundations of experiential learning, its intimate mechanism, and very essence.



  As an empiric EL practitioner I had no clue about most of the other authors and their stunning thoughts about EL. 

  Funny enough, reading them made me experience namely what they promoted: understanding what happened. Indeed, it took me 10 years to find out EL theories, but I can now feel that I got a full grasp of the big picture, though much is still to come.



  I originally thought of simply listing these citations as they represented an already valuable finding. As my searches went on, my AI buddies triggered curiosity, and about situational learning and then about simulations, I got no real citations, but more some ultra condensed summaries, still are they fundamental for embracing EL. 

  The day was ending but I suddenly remembered that I was (am) a big fan of movies having a close or a far connexion with simulations and experience learning.  I attempted to list these movies some time ago, like The Game or Truman Show so that I could illustrate EL with well-known references.

  I asked generative AI LLM to dig up and got an unexpected list of movies punchlines with most of them I forgot! Extending it to literature just nailed it !\

  Eventually out of the many citations, quotes and punchlines obtained from verified AI search, I asked it to extract and summarize some key principles. Here is the first condensed version of all quotes obtained. 


  * You learn by doing, not by watching. 

  * First do it, then understand it. 

  * The path is made by walking. 

  * Safe failure is the fastest teacher. 

  * You know more than you can say, so practice to reveal it.

  * Don't wait until you know how — start, and learning will follow.

  * When you teach, you also learn. We gain new skills by trying what we cannot yet do. 

  * Mistakes are good teachers, especially when it is safe to make them.
    We learn best by working with others in real situations.

  I reordered these principles and refined them again up to a one single tagline, what do we have now ? 


  > Start before you're ready, 

  > learn by doing - together, 

  > fail safely, 

  > reflect deeply, and mentor others

  > to seal your competence."




  Though it doesn't fully match it, there are quite some similarities with Kolb Experiential Learning Theory itself : 


  * Concrete Experience - feeling

  * Reflective Observation - watching

  * Active Experimentation - doing

  * Abstract Conceptualization - thinking




  The 'start before you're ready' first step could be definetly questioned: how can one start the learning process from scratch? Do one needs a minimal corpus of knowledge or an observation phase - watch how others cope with the activity sought ?


  > "Learning would be exceedingly laborious, not to mention hazardous, if people had to rely solely on the effects of their own actions to inform them what to do." — Albert Bandura, Social Learning Theory (1977), p. 22 




  I'll dedicate a separate post to this later: What should an EL practitioner provide to students before initiating the learning process?

  This question has haunted most of my EL experiences and experiments. Having worked with more than 500 students over 10 years, I still don't have a definite, documented answer. I willingly set it aside and kept walking along the road.


  An idea came to light : if these are process steps then, why not regrouping all the original quotes/citations/punchlines found into corresponding topics ?

  What are we waiting for ? Let's start now !
