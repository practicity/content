## The Event
The World Health Assembly (WHA) simulation is an annual event hosted at York University, designed as an experiential learning opportunity for global health students. It emulates the real World Health Assembly held every May in Geneva, allowing students to step into the roles and proceedings of the World Health Organization.

## Why It Matters
As global health students, participants rely on the vision and hope embodied by the WHO. The simulation helps them understand deeply how the organization functions—its weaknesses, but also its incredible strengths—and the promise it holds for a collaborative, multilateral world.

## Skills Beyond the Classroom
Faculty emphasize that experiential education builds soft skills rarely acquired in lectures. Students learn how to argue persuasively, how to write policies, and how to apply these competencies in their daily professional activities. The organizers want students to leave recognizing that they are already leaders, true agents of change, and catalysts for radical transformation.

## An Experiential Learning Perspective
This simulation is a textbook example of situated learning: knowledge is acquired within an authentic community of practice that mirrors real diplomatic proceedings. The most important lesson is that abstract concepts—global governance, multilateralism, public health policy—become tangible only when learners must negotiate, draft, and defend them under realistic constraints. For other practitioners, the strength lies in identity formation: students stop seeing themselves as passive recipients and begin acting as professionals. It is highly reproducible: any institution can stage a model assembly by assigning country delegations, providing authentic procedural rules, supplying policy briefs, and scaffolding reflection. Combining intellectual rigor with genuine enjoyment, it demonstrates how engagement and motivation amplify deep learning."
